  Shape-by-Shape Verdicts

  Circle — The star performer. Near-optimal or above floor on essentially everything.
  - Fill: 112-128% (faster than rect-AA due to more cache-sequential scanlines)
  - 1px stroke opaque: 193-222% (Bresenham 8-fold symmetry = 1/4 the setup of rect-AA's 4 line segments)
  - Fill+stroke: 102-188%
  - Verdict: No work needed. Already beyond theoretical floor.

  Horizontal line — Speed demon. 197-407% for opaque, 124-332% for semi. Sequential memset writes are the fastest possible operation. This is the ceiling, not the floor.

  Vertical line — Cache penalty is real but modest. 1px opaque at 58-61% (perpendicular to scanlines). Thick strokes become wide horizontal spans and perform well (104-370%).

  Diagonal line — Middle ground. 1px opaque: 61-75%. Thick: 26-90%, improving with size as fixed overhead amortizes.

  Roundrect-AA — Excellent fill, catastrophic 1px stroke.
  - Fill: 103-140% — faster than rect-AA, likely due to the rounded-corner scanline being more cache-sequential
  - Fill+stroke (thick): 121-193% — above floor
  - 1px stroke: 5-20% — the single worst result in the entire engine. At szS, 6.07µs per shape regardless of size. This is dominated by a massive fixed per-shape cost (path reconstruction?). The proximity barely improves with size (5% ->
  11% -> 20%), meaning the fixed cost is enormous.

  Arc — Expensive 1px stroke, decent fill.
  - Fill opaque: 36-64% — scanline intersection computation is significant overhead
  - 1px stroke opaque: 16-19% constant across all angles and sizes — Bresenham traces the full circle regardless of arc angle, so the cost is pure algorithmic overhead with almost no per-pixel benefit
  - Thick stroke/fill+stroke: improves with size, reaching 80-99% at szL

  Rect-rot — The optimization goldmine.
  - Fill opaque: 23-55% — DDA scanline computation overhead is significant for small shapes, improves with size
  - Fill semi: 81-120% — semi cost (4.39 ns/px) dwarfs rotation overhead, so proximity is good
  - 1px stroke: 39-89% — reasonable, improves with size
  - Thick semi stroke: 19-22% across ALL sizes — catastrophically slow. Does not improve with size. At szL swL: 369µs actual vs 72µs floor = 5.1x overhead
  - Fill-opaque + stroke-semi: 12-35% — the worst combined path. At swL szL: 383µs actual vs 63µs floor = 6.1x overhead

  Roundrect-rot — Similar to rect-rot, slightly better.
  - Fill opaque: 19-83% — rotation overhead, but reaches 83% at szL
  - Thick fill+stroke: 22-95% — improves steadily
  - Thick semi stroke: 54-121% — noticeably better than rect-rot semi stroke

  Key Patterns

  1. Semi-transparency is the great equalizer. When alpha blending dominates (4.39+ ns/px), shape overhead becomes relatively invisible. Many shapes achieve 80-100%+ proximity for semi ops at large sizes.
  2. Small shapes expose fixed overhead. Proximity universally drops at szS. If a shape has a 2µs per-shape setup cost, and the actual pixel work is 0.5µs, you're at 20% proximity no matter how fast the pixel loop is.
  3. 1px strokes are disproportionately expensive for complex shapes. They trace complex paths with per-pixel computation but touch relatively few pixels, so the computation-to-pixel ratio is terrible.
  4. Rect-rot thick semi stroke is a genuine bug-level performance issue. At 19-22% proximity that doesn't improve with size, something is fundamentally wrong — possibly re-computing rotation per scanline, or an O(n^2) inner loop, or
  missing a fast path that rect-rot thick opaque stroke has
